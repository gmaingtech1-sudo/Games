The Eight-Ball Open

Rules

The game follows the official rules of the World Eightball Pool Federation (the abridged version, January 2020). Here's how they play out on the table.

Winning a frame

There are seven reds, seven yellows and the black eight-ball. Pot all of your colour, in any order and any pockets, then pot the eight-ball to win. You can pot more than one of your colour in a shot, but the eight-ball needs a shot of its own.

The break

  • A coin toss decides who breaks the first frame. After that, players take turns to break.
  • You break from baulk: the cue ball's centre must be behind the baulk line, the line across the bottom fifth of the table.
  • A fair break pots at least one colour, or drives at least four balls to a cushion.
  • Not a fair break: the balls are re-racked and your opponent breaks instead, with two visits.
  • Eight-ball potted on the break: re-rack, and the same player breaks again.
  • Cue ball potted on a fair break: your turn ends and your opponent plays from baulk.

Deciding colours

  • Until colours are decided the table is open, and you can play at either colour (never the eight-ball first).
  • Pot a colour on the break and you choose your colour. Choose one you potted and it's yours. Choose one you didn't, and you must pot it with your next shot to be on it.
  • After the break, potting balls of one colour puts you on that colour. Pot both and you choose.
  • Colours are never decided on a foul. Once decided, they're yours for the rest of the frame.

A legal shot

The cue ball must hit a ball you're on first. Then you must either pot a ball you're on, or make any ball (cue ball included) touch a cushion. Pot one of yours legally and you play again.

If you're in a total snooker, with no straight line to any part of any ball you're on, you only need to hit one. The game spots this for you.

Fouls and two visits

After a standard foul your opponent gets two visits. If they miss on the first, they simply play again from where the balls stopped. A foul on either visit passes the turn back.

  • Potting the cue ball (an in-off). The next player plays from baulk.
  • Hitting the wrong ball first, or missing everything.
  • Potting one of your opponent's balls.
  • No ball potted and nothing reaching a cushion.
  • Playing into a ball the cue ball is touching (see below).
  • Taking more than 60 seconds (a non-standard foul): two visits, from where the cue ball lies or from baulk.

Other fouls go on: the cue ball stays where it stops.

Foul snookers

If, after a foul, you can't hit both edges of any of your balls in a straight line, you're foul snookered. For your first shot you can nominate one of your opponent's balls (or the eight-ball) to count as yours, and potting it is fine. Or you can take the cue ball into baulk. Pot a nominated eight-ball before you're on it and you lose the frame.

On the eight-ball and foul snookered? Pot the nominated ball, the eight-ball, or both, and you win.

Touching balls

If the cue ball is touching another ball, you must play away from it at more than 90°. If it touches several and one of them is yours, playing away from that one lets you play into the others.

Losing the frame

  • Potting the eight-ball on a foul.
  • Potting the eight-ball while any of your colour is still on the table.
  • Potting the eight-ball in the same shot as your last ball.

Time and stalemates

You have 60 seconds for each shot, with a warning at 30. The clock can be switched off in Settings. If both players go three turns in a row without the cue ball touching another ball, it's a stalemate and the frame is replayed with the same player breaking.

Left out

Rules about the lag, conduct at the table (smoking, drinks, coaching, leaving the area), push shots, double hits, jump shots, balls leaving the table and balls falling on their own can't happen on a phone, so they're not in the game. Foul jaw snookers (where the jaw of a pocket blocks the shot) aren't detected.
