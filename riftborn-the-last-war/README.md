# Riftborn: The Last War

*The World, the Factions and the Core Rules*

A tabletop miniatures wargame for two players, played with D6 dice, a tape measure and two armies on a 48" × 48" battlefield. Four hundred years after the Great Shattering broke the walls between realities, every Rift is drifting into one colossal wound in the sky, the Maw, and every army is marching to reach it first.

This book brings together the World Guide, the Faction Guide and Core Rules Part 1.

# Part One: The World

## Introduction

This part sets out the universe of Riftborn: The Last War. It explains how reality was built, how it broke, and why every army now marches toward the same burning wound in the sky. It is the foundation that the rules, factions, missions and artwork will be built on.

Everything here is written to serve the tabletop. Rift Energy becomes a game mechanic, the key locations become battlefields, and the open mysteries become hooks for future expansions and campaigns. Where it leaves something unexplained, that is deliberate: a universe with secrets gives players room to tell their own stories.

> *Every world we ever knew was a page in the same book. Then someone tore the binding.* — Fragment recovered from Veilspire

## 1. The Universe

### The Strata

Reality is not one world but countless worlds, each layered over the next like pages in a book. Scholars call these layers the **Strata**. Each Stratum is a complete reality with its own skies, laws of nature, peoples and history. In one, iron cities spread across a frozen continent. In another, living oceans think slow thoughts. In others there is nothing but ash and silence.

For most of history, the inhabitants of each Stratum had no idea the others existed. A soldier on one world could stand on the exact spot where, a single layer away, a monster slept beneath an alien sea, and neither would ever know.

### The Veil

What kept the Strata apart was the **Veil**: a barrier woven between every layer of reality. It was not a wall that could be seen or touched. It was closer to a law of nature, a rule that said each reality must stay where it belonged.

The Veil was not natural. It was built.

### The Vaultwrights

The builders were the **Vaultwrights**, an ancient race that existed before any of the current peoples of the Strata. Almost nothing is known about them. They left no paintings of themselves, no written histories and no graves. What they did leave are their machines: vast structures of black metal and pale crystal, buried deep beneath the surface of dozens of worlds, still humming with power after uncounted ages.

Why the Vaultwrights built the Veil is one of the great questions of the universe. Some believe it was an act of kindness, to let each reality grow in peace. Others believe the Veil was a prison, and that the Vaultwrights were keeping something locked away. Then, long before recorded history, the Vaultwrights vanished, leaving their machines behind.

### The Age of the Veil

While the Veil held, each Stratum developed alone. Empires rose and fell. Wars were fought, won and forgotten. None of it ever touched another reality. This long era of isolation is now remembered as the Age of the Veil, and to those who live in the shattered universe of today, it seems almost like a dream of peace.

## 2. The Great Shattering

### The Varkonian Discovery

Of all the peoples of the Strata, few were as ambitious as the **Varkonian Dominion**. From their fortress-world of Karrath Prime, the Varkonians had conquered their entire reality through discipline, armour and war machines. When there was nothing left to conquer, they began to dig.

Beneath Karrath Prime, Varkonian engineers uncovered the largest Vaultwright structure ever found: a machine the size of a mountain range, built around a single pulsing core. Ancient inscriptions, partly translated, named it the **Heart Engine**. The Varkonians believed it was a power source capable of fuelling their empire forever.

They were not entirely wrong.

### The Night of Breaking Glass

Four hundred years ago, after decades of work, the Varkonian high command ordered the Heart Engine to be awakened. Witnesses who survived describe a sound like the whole sky being struck, and then a second sound, like every window in existence breaking at once. This is why that moment is now called the **Night of Breaking Glass**.

The Heart Engine did not simply release power. It pulled against the Veil itself, and the Veil, which had held for longer than anyone could measure, cracked. Not just on Karrath Prime, but across every Stratum at the same instant.

> *We were told we would light the empire for ten thousand years. Instead we set the universe on fire.* — Varkonian engineer, final log entry

### Something on the Other Side

The official Varkonian records blame the Shattering on a calculation error. But there are other accounts. Some survivors of the excavation swear that in the moments before the Veil broke, the Heart Engine was already active, and that it was not answering their commands. Others speak of a pressure from beyond the Veil, as though something on the far side had been pushing against it for a very long time and the Varkonians simply opened the door.

The truth remains unknown. What is certain is that the Varkonian Dominion has carried the blame ever since, and it has never forgiven those who say so.

### The Aftermath

The results were catastrophic and immediate:

- **Worlds collided.** Where the Veil failed, Strata bled into one another. Mountains appeared in the middle of oceans. Cities found themselves half-buried in alien jungles. Entire peoples woke up beneath strange stars.

- **The Riftspawn arrived.** Through the cracks poured the **Riftspawn**: alien creatures from realities never meant to touch any other. They were not an army with a plan. They were hunger and instinct, and they spread across every world they reached.

- **The dead rose.** On Strata that were destroyed in the collisions, something unnatural happened. The dead did not stay dead. Twisted by leaking Rift Energy, they rose again as the **Hollowed**, warriors with no memory of who they had been, driven by powers no one understood.

Within a single night, the Age of the Veil was over. The universe of Riftborn had begun.

## 3. The Rifts & Rift Energy

### What Is a Rift?

The cracks left behind by the Shattering are called **Rifts**. A Rift is an open wound between realities: a tear in the air that shows glimpses of another world through its edges. Some are no larger than a doorway. Others stretch across the horizon.

Rifts are unstable. They appear without warning, drift slowly across the land, and close as suddenly as they opened. A valley that was safe yesterday may hold a Rift today, and whatever has crawled out of it. Armies plan their campaigns around Rift sightings the way sailors plan around storms.

### Rift Energy

Through every Rift leaks **Rift Energy**: raw reality, unshaped and wild. It shows itself as shimmering violet light, a metallic taste in the air, and a sense of pressure behind the eyes.

Rift Energy is the most valuable resource in the universe. It can be harvested, stored and channelled, and every faction has found its own way to use it:

- It powers weapons that strike harder than any natural force.

- It fuels war machines and engines far beyond what ordinary power can drive.

- It awakens psychic gifts in those who can survive its touch.

- It can open small, controlled Rifts, allowing warriors to step across a battlefield in an instant.

### The Price of Power

Rift Energy has a terrible cost. Too much of it changes the user. Bodies twist and mutate, minds fill with voices from other realities, and those who push too far can be torn apart from the inside or lost entirely to the Rift. Every commander who draws on Rift Energy is gambling, and every army has stories of the soldier who reached for a little more power and was never seen again.

> **How This Shapes the Game**
>
> Rift Energy will become a core mechanic in the rules. Players will be able to gather and spend Rift Energy to unlock powerful abilities, but pushing too hard will carry risk, from damage to unpredictable effects.
>
> This creates the central tension of every battle: **play it safe, or risk everything for power.**

## 4. Timeline

Riftborn scholars date events from the Night of Breaking Glass. Years before it are marked **BS** (Before the Shattering) and years after are marked **AS** (After the Shattering). The Last War takes place in roughly 400 AS.

### The Age of the Veil (unknown – 0 BS)

- The Vaultwrights build the Veil and the great machines beneath many worlds.

- The Vaultwrights vanish. No record explains where or why.

- Each Stratum develops in isolation. On one world, the Varkonian Dominion rises and conquers its entire reality.

- Varkonian engineers discover the Heart Engine beneath Karrath Prime and spend decades studying it.

### The Night of Breaking Glass (0 AS)

- The Varkonian Dominion awakens the Heart Engine.

- The Veil cracks across every Stratum at once.

- Worlds collide, the Riftspawn emerge, and the first Hollowed rise from the dead of shattered realities.

### The Drowning Years (0 – 100 AS)

- A century of chaos as Strata collapse into one another, a process survivors call "drowning".

- Billions die. Whole civilisations disappear in a single day.

- The Riftspawn spread across countless worlds, adapting to every new environment they reach.

- Surviving peoples begin to learn about Rift Energy, often at great cost.

### The Fractured Accords (100 – 380 AS)

- Survivors from different realities begin to meet, trade and form fragile alliances.

- The surviving factions grow strong: the Varkonian Dominion, the Nexari Collective, the Ironclad Clans and the mysterious Veilkeepers.

- Treaties are signed and broken. Borders shift with every new Rift.

- The Hollowed gather on dead worlds, growing in number and purpose.

- Peace, where it exists, is temporary and expensive.

### The Last War (380 AS – present)

- Observers notice the Rifts are no longer moving at random. They are drifting together.

- At the point where they meet, a single colossal Rift begins to form. It is named **the Maw**.

- Ancient Vaultwright texts suggest that whoever controls the Maw could rewrite reality itself: restore the Veil, reshape the Strata, or end everything.

- Every faction realises the same thing at once. The Accords collapse, and every army marches toward the Maw.

> *There will be no peace after this war. There will only be whoever is left standing at the Maw.* — Ironclad saying

## 5. Key Locations

The Last War is fought across many worlds. These are among the most important, and each offers a distinct battlefield for games of Riftborn.

### Karrath Prime

The fortress-world and capital of the Varkonian Dominion, and the place where the Shattering began. Its surface is covered in iron citadels, weapon foundries and endless parade grounds. At its heart lies the dormant Heart Engine, sealed beneath layers of armour and guarded by the Dominion's most loyal troops. The Varkonians claim it is safe. Few outsiders believe them.

**On the tabletop:** fortified bunkers, trenches, gun emplacements and ruined industrial terrain.

### The Maw

The growing super-Rift at the centre of the Last War. It hangs in the sky like a vast eye, ringed with violet lightning, and the land beneath it is a broken patchwork of a hundred different realities pressed together. Rift Energy here is so strong that the laws of nature bend. Time slips, gravity shifts and the dead do not always stay down.

**On the tabletop:** the ultimate battlefield, with unstable terrain and heavy Rift Energy effects.

### Ashen Hollow

A world that was destroyed during the Drowning Years. Its cities are grey ruins under a sky that never fully brightens, and its soil is thick with ash. It is now the gathering place of the Hollowed, who return here for reasons no living creature understands. Those who enter speak of whispers in the ash and figures that move at the edge of sight.

**On the tabletop:** ruined cities, ash fields, graveyards and haunted ground.

### The Gearfall Wastes

A vast machine graveyard where the wreckage of countless realities has piled up over centuries: shattered war engines, fallen starships and the bones of Vaultwright structures. It is ruled by the **Nexari Collective**, whose robotic legions harvest the wreckage for parts and knowledge. Every ruin hides something valuable, which means every ruin is worth fighting over.

**On the tabletop:** scrap mountains, crashed machines and dense, cluttered cover.

### Veilspire

A floating citadel that drifts between realities, belonging to the secretive **Veilkeepers**. It never stays in one Stratum for long, and only those invited can find it. The Veilkeepers claim to be the guardians of what is left of the Veil. What they truly want, and what they know about the Shattering, remains hidden behind the walls of their wandering fortress.

**On the tabletop:** elevated platforms, teleport gates and strange architecture.

## 6. Mysteries & Story Hooks

These open questions are left unanswered on purpose. They give players something to wonder about and give future expansions, campaigns and storylines a place to begin.

### Who, or what, was really behind the Shattering?

Was it a Varkonian mistake, or did something beyond the Veil use the Heart Engine to break free? If something did come through, where is it now?

### Why did the Vaultwrights disappear?

Did they leave, die out, or seal themselves away? Some of their machines are still running. Someone, or something, may still be operating them.

### The Riftspawn are evolving, but into what?

The first Riftspawn were mindless beasts. Recent reports describe creatures that set ambushes, use tactics and seem to follow a single will. Something may be guiding them.

### The Veilkeepers know more than they say.

They appeared soon after the Shattering, already knowing how to travel between realities. How did they learn so quickly? And are they trying to repair the Veil, or control it?

### What lies at the heart of the Maw?

Every faction believes the Maw holds the power to remake reality. But no one who has entered it has ever returned to say what is inside.

# Part Two: The Factions

## Introduction

Part One explained how reality broke. This part introduces the powers that fight over what is left.

Six factions march toward the Maw. The first chapter gives a short introduction to each of them, so players can see the shape of the whole war. The next two chapters tell the full stories of the two launch factions, the **Varkonian Dominion** and the **Riftspawn**, from their origins to the way they fight on the tabletop.

The launch factions were chosen because they are opposites. The Varkonians are an army of discipline, armour and firepower. The Riftspawn are a tide of claws, hunger and evolution. Together they create the classic "army against the swarm" battle, which makes them ideal for learning the game.

> *The Dominion counts its soldiers. The swarm does not count at all.* — Varkonian field saying

## The Six Factions

A short introduction to every faction of the Last War. The four factions not covered in full here are ready to be expanded in future steps and expansions.

### 1. The Varkonian Dominion

*Armoured soldiers and war machines*

The oldest surviving empire in the Strata, ruled from the fortress-world of Karrath Prime. The Varkonians awakened the Heart Engine and are blamed by every other faction for the Great Shattering.

- **What they want:** To reach the Maw first and prove that the Dominion can repair what it broke, on its own terms.

- **How they fight:** Disciplined battle lines, heavy armour, long-range firepower and towering war machines.

- **On the tabletop:** Tough and reliable. Strong at range and holding ground, but slower than most enemies.

### 2. The Riftspawn

*Alien creatures and evolving monsters*

Creatures from realities that were never meant to touch any other. They poured through the Rifts on the Night of Breaking Glass and have been spreading ever since.

- **What they want:** To feed, to grow, and to reach the Maw, which draws them like a beacon.

- **How they fight:** Overwhelming numbers, speed and savage close combat. They evolve during battle.

- **On the tabletop:** Fast and numerous. Deadly in melee, weak at range, and individually fragile.

### 3. The Hollowed

*Corrupted warriors and supernatural powers*

The dead of shattered worlds, raised again by Rift Energy. They remember nothing of who they were, but they move with a purpose no living creature understands.

- **What they want:** Unknown. They gather at Ashen Hollow and march toward the Maw in silence.

- **How they fight:** Relentless warriors that are hard to destroy, supported by dark supernatural abilities.

- **On the tabletop:** Slow but almost unbreakable. Fallen models may rise again.

### 4. The Nexari Collective

*Advanced robots and automated weapons*

A machine civilisation that rules the Gearfall Wastes. The Nexari harvest the wreckage of a hundred realities, learning from every ruin they strip.

- **What they want:** To claim the Vaultwright machines and the knowledge hidden inside the Maw.

- **How they fight:** Precise robotic infantry, drones and automated gun platforms that link their targeting together.

- **On the tabletop:** Accurate and coordinated. Weaker when their network is broken.

### 5. The Ironclad Clans

*Armoured warriors who specialise in melee*

Proud warrior clans whose world was drowned in the Shattering. They survived by building massive armour and refusing to die.

- **What they want:** Glory, revenge, and a new homeland won by strength.

- **How they fight:** Heavily armoured shock troops who charge straight into the enemy.

- **On the tabletop:** The best close-combat infantry in the game. Few models, each one very tough.

### 6. The Veilkeepers

*Psychic powers and teleportation*

A secretive order that appeared soon after the Shattering, already able to travel between realities. Their floating citadel, Veilspire, drifts from Stratum to Stratum.

- **What they want:** They claim they want to restore the Veil. Their true goal is hidden.

- **How they fight:** Psychic attacks, shields and teleportation through small, controlled Rifts.

- **On the tabletop:** Elite and mobile. Powerful abilities, but very few models.

## The Varkonian Dominion

> *Order is the only wall that never falls.* — First Article of the Iron Oath

### Origins

Long before the Shattering, Karrath Prime was a world of warring city-states, poor in resources and harsh in climate. The Varkonians were one people among many, until a general named **Aurek Varkos** united them through discipline rather than numbers. His soldiers were fewer than their enemies, but they marched in step, followed orders without question, and never broke formation.

Within a century, Varkos's heirs had conquered all of Karrath Prime. Within a thousand years, the Dominion ruled its entire reality. Every world in that Stratum was turned into a fortress, a foundry or a barracks.

### The Heart Engine and the Blame

When there was nothing left to conquer, the Dominion began to dig. Beneath Karrath Prime they found the Heart Engine, and after decades of study the high command ordered it awakened. The result was the Night of Breaking Glass.

Officially, the Dominion admits to a "calculation error" and nothing more. Inside the Dominion, however, the Shattering is never spoken of as a mistake. It is called **the Debt**. Every Varkonian soldier is taught that the Dominion broke the world and that only the Dominion has the discipline to fix it. This belief drives them toward the Maw with grim determination.

To the other factions, this sounds like arrogance. Many believe the Varkonians do not want to repair reality at all, but to rule what comes after.

### Society and Culture

- **The Iron Oath.** Every citizen swears the Iron Oath at the age of service. It binds them to obey the chain of command above their own life. Breaking the Oath is the greatest shame a Varkonian can suffer.

- **The Ledger.** The Dominion keeps a record of every life lost since the Shattering, called the Ledger of the Debt. Soldiers believe their deaths are written into it as payment toward restoring the universe.

- **Rank is everything.** Varkonians are known by rank before name. Uniforms, armour markings and even the colour of a soldier's visor show their place in the chain of command.

- **Machines are honoured.** War machines are given names, histories and crews who serve them for life. Losing a machine in battle is mourned like the loss of a commander.

### Leadership

The Dominion is ruled by the **Iron Regent**, a title held by the descendants of Aurek Varkos. The current Regent, **Severa Varkos**, took the throne during the Fractured Accords and is the one who declared the march on the Maw. She is cold, patient and utterly certain that only she can end the Last War.

Below the Regent stand the **Lord-Marshals**, each commanding an entire Legion. On the battlefield, armies are led by **Commanders** and **Field Captains**, whose orders keep the battle line together.

### The Varkonian Military

A Varkonian army is built like a machine, with every part in its place:

- **Legionaries** form the core: armoured infantry in disciplined lines, armed with volt rifles.

- **Bastion Guards** carry heavy shields and hold key ground against any assault.

- **Breacher squads** carry short-range weapons and flame projectors to clear enemies from cover.

- **Specialist teams** crew heavy weapons, from long-range cannons to Rift-core artillery.

- **War machines** walk, roll or hover into battle, carrying the firepower of a whole squad.

### The Varkonians and Rift Energy

After the Shattering, the Dominion swore never to let Rift Energy run wild again. Varkonian engineers trap it inside armoured containers called **Rift-cores**, which power their heaviest weapons and machines. This makes their use of Rift Energy safer than most factions, but also slower and less flexible. A Varkonian commander rarely gambles on power. They prefer to calculate.

### Goals in the Last War

The Dominion's plan is simple: reach the Maw, seal it with Vaultwright technology recovered from beneath Karrath Prime, and become the power that saved reality. Whether sealing the Maw would truly restore the Veil, or simply give the Dominion control over every Stratum, is a question the Iron Regent does not answer.

> **How the Varkonian Dominion Plays**
>
> **Strengths:** high armour, strong ranged firepower, reliable morale, and commanders whose orders boost nearby units.
>
> **Weaknesses:** slower movement, fewer models, and expensive units. Being caught in melee by a larger force is dangerous.
>
> **Playstyle:** hold a strong position, build a firing line, and destroy the enemy before they arrive. Use war machines and Bastion Guards to anchor objectives.
>
> **Best for:** players who like planning, positioning and firepower.

## The Riftspawn

> *You cannot negotiate with a storm. You cannot reason with the swarm.* — Veilkeeper teaching

### Origins

No one knows exactly where the Riftspawn came from. They did not arrive from a single world but from many, all of them realities so hostile that nothing like human life could survive there. When the Veil cracked, the creatures of those realities found doors open for the first time in history, and they came through hungry.

At first the Riftspawn were scattered and mindless: packs of beasts that attacked anything that moved. That is no longer true.

### The Brood

Every Riftspawn belongs to a **Brood**: a swarm of creatures connected by instinct and scent. A Brood behaves almost like a single animal. When one part of it is attacked, the rest react. When one part finds food, the rest follow.

Riftspawn **feed on Rift Energy**. They are drawn to Rifts the way animals are drawn to water, and each time they feed they change. A Brood that has fed well grows larger, faster and more dangerous. A Brood that has fed on the energy of the Maw itself becomes something new.

### The Forms of the Riftspawn

- **Swarmers** are the smallest and most common: fast, clawed hunters that attack in huge numbers.

- **Stalkers** are camouflaged ambush predators that strike from hiding and vanish again.

- **Spitters** launch corrosive bile from range, the closest thing the swarm has to artillery.

- **Brutes** are hulking, armoured beasts that smash through battle lines.

- **Brood-Mothers** are huge creatures at the heart of each Brood. They spawn new creatures and drive the swarm forward.

- **Apex Horrors** are rare, monstrous giants that have fed on so much Rift Energy they can bend reality around them.

### The Evolving Threat

In the last twenty years, reports from the front lines have changed. Riftspawn now set ambushes. They retreat to draw enemies into traps. They attack supply lines instead of soldiers. Some Broods appear to work together across hundreds of miles, as if following a single command.

Varkonian scholars call this unseen force **the Choir**, after the low, layered humming that survivors report hearing before a major attack. Whether the Choir is a creature, a mind, or something that came through the Rifts on the Night of Breaking Glass, no one knows.

### Goals in the Last War

The Riftspawn do not make plans in any way other factions understand. But every Brood in the Strata is now moving toward the Maw. The greatest fear of every other faction is simple: if the swarm reaches the Maw and feeds on it, what will it become?

> **How the Riftspawn Play**
>
> **Strengths:** speed, numbers, cheap units and devastating close combat. They can evolve during battle by feeding on Rift Energy.
>
> **Weaknesses:** low armour, weak shooting, and most individual creatures die easily. Without a large creature nearby, the swarm is harder to control.
>
> **Playstyle:** close the distance fast, surround the enemy and overwhelm them before their firepower takes over. Use Stalkers and Swarmers to tie up enemy units while Brutes break their lines.
>
> **Best for:** players who like aggressive, fast and unpredictable games.

## The Eternal Enemies

Of all the conflicts in the Last War, none is older or more bitter than the war between the Dominion and the swarm.

### Why They Fight

The first Riftspawn to come through the Veil emerged on Karrath Prime itself, pouring out of the cracks around the Heart Engine. For the Varkonians, the swarm is the living proof of the Debt: a punishment they brought upon themselves. For the Riftspawn, Karrath Prime holds the greatest source of Rift Energy outside the Maw. They have never stopped trying to reach it.

Four hundred years of war have shaped both sides. The Dominion's fortresses, flame weapons and tactics were all built to stop the swarm. In turn, the swarm has evolved creatures built to break Varkonian armour.

### Famous Battles

- **The Siege of the Engine Gate (0 AS).** On the Night of Breaking Glass, the Bastion Guard held the tunnels around the Heart Engine for nine days while the swarm poured out. Fewer than one in ten survived. The Bastion Guard still carry nine marks on their shields in memory.

- **The Burning of Kesh (112 AS).** When a Brood-Mother nested in the city of Kesh, the Dominion burned the whole city to stop it, along with everyone still inside. The decision is still argued over in Varkonian war colleges.

- **The Silent Retreat (391 AS).** A Riftspawn army withdrew from a battle it was winning, drawing a Varkonian Legion into a canyon where three more Broods were waiting. It was the first time the Dominion faced the Choir, and the first time they admitted the swarm could think.

> **Battle Ideas for Players**
>
> **Hold the Line:** the Varkonians defend a fortified position while the Riftspawn try to break through.
>
> **The Nest:** the Varkonians must destroy a Brood-Mother before the swarm grows too large.
>
> **Rift Surge:** both sides race to control a Rift as it opens in the middle of the battlefield.

# Part Three: The Core Rules

## Introduction

This part contains Part 1 of the core rules for Riftborn: The Last War. It covers everything players need to move their armies, shoot, and fight in close combat.

Core Rules Part 2 will add morale, commanders, terrain and the Rift Energy mechanic. Together, the two parts form the complete core rules.

### Design Goals

- **Deep but accessible.** A new player should be able to learn the basics in one game, while experienced players discover tactics over many.

- **Always involved.** Players take turns activating one unit at a time, so no one waits long for their next decision.

- **Fast resolution.** Attacks use only two rolls: one to hit and one to save.

- **D6 only.** Every roll in the game uses standard six-sided dice.

### What You Need

- Two armies of Riftborn miniatures, with their unit cards.

- A battlefield, ideally 48" x 48" for 20–40 models per side (or 60" x 44" for larger games).

- Terrain: ruins, walls, hills, forests and Rift features.

- A tape measure in inches.

- A handful of six-sided dice (D6).

- Activation tokens to mark units that have acted this round.

> **Note on Example Profiles**
>
> Example units and weapons in this part show how the rules work. Final profiles, abilities and points costs for every unit will be delivered with the Army steps.

## 1. Core Concepts

### Models and Units

Each miniature is a **model**. Models fight together in groups called **units**. A unit can be a squad of ten soldiers, a pack of creatures, a single hero, or one large war machine.

Every model in a unit must stay within **2" of at least one other model** from the same unit. This is called **coherency**. If a unit ends any move out of coherency, the controlling player must remove models until it is back in coherency.

### Measuring

All distances are in inches and are measured between the closest points of the models' bases. For models without a base, measure from the closest part of the model. Players may measure distances at any time.

When a rule refers to a distance to a **unit**, measure to the closest model in that unit.

### Line of Sight

A model can see a target if a straight line can be drawn from any part of its base to any part of the target model's base without passing through terrain that blocks sight or through other models' bases (other than models in its own unit). When in doubt, players should agree before the game, or roll off.

### Dice

- Every roll uses a **D6**. A **D3** is a D6 halved and rounded up (1–2 = 1, 3–4 = 2, 5–6 = 3).

- A target such as **4+** means you need to roll 4 or higher.

- An unmodified roll of **1 always fails**, and an unmodified roll of **6 always succeeds** (except a save that needs 7+, which cannot be made).

- Modifiers are added together, but the total can never be more than **+1 or –1** on any roll.

- A dice can never be **re-rolled** more than once. Re-rolls happen before modifiers are applied.

- **Roll-off:** both players roll a D6; the highest wins. Re-roll ties.

## 2. Unit Profiles

Each unit has a **unit card** showing its stats, weapons and special abilities.

## 3. The Battle Round

A game of Riftborn is played over **five battle rounds**. Each round has three phases:

1.  **Initiative Phase.** Both players roll off. The winner chooses who activates first this round.

2.  **Activation Phase.** Players take turns activating one unit at a time until every unit has activated.

3.  **End Phase.** Check morale, score objectives and remove activation tokens. (Morale and objectives are covered in Core Rules Part 2.)

### Alternating Activations

Starting with the player chosen in the Initiative Phase, players take turns choosing one of their units that has not yet activated this round. That unit performs its activation, then receives an **activation token**. Then the other player activates a unit.

If one player runs out of units to activate, the other player activates their remaining units one after another.

> **Why Alternating Activations?**
>
> Instead of moving your whole army and then waiting while your opponent does the same, both players are involved all the time. Every activation is a decision: strike now, or wait and react to what your opponent does?

### Actions

When a unit activates, it may take **two actions** from the list below. A unit may take the same action twice only if it is **Move**.

| **Action** | **What the unit does**                                                          |
|:-----------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Move**   | Move each model up to its Move stat (see section 4).                            |
| **Shoot**  | Attack an enemy unit with ranged weapons (see section 5).                       |
| **Charge** | Move into combat with an enemy unit and attack it (see section 6).              |
| **Fight**  | Attack an enemy unit the unit is already engaged with (see section 6).          |
| **Aim**    | Take careful aim: +1 to hit with this unit's next Shoot action this activation. |
| **Hold**   | Take no further action. Useful when a unit should stay in position.             |

**Engaged Units**

A unit with any model within **1" of an enemy model** is **engaged**. An engaged unit may only take the **Fight** or **Withdraw** actions (see section 4), plus any actions allowed by special rules.

## 4. Movement

### The Move Action

When a unit takes a Move action, each model may move up to its **Move** stat in inches, in any direction. Models can turn freely and do not need to move in a straight line.

- Models cannot move through other models (friendly or enemy), except over their own unit.

- Models cannot move within **1" of an enemy model**, except by charging.

- The unit must end its move in coherency.

A unit that takes **two Move actions** in one activation is moving at full speed. It cannot Shoot or Charge in that activation.

### Terrain and Movement

Terrain features are described in detail in Core Rules Part 2. For now, use these basic rules:

- **Open ground:** no penalty.

- **Difficult ground** (rubble, forests, shallow water): each inch moved counts as 2 inches.

- **Impassable terrain** (solid walls, deep chasms): models cannot move through it.

- **Climbing:** moving up or down counts toward the distance moved.

### Withdraw

An engaged unit can use **both of its actions** to Withdraw. Each model moves up to its Move stat and must end more than 1" away from all enemy models.

Before the withdrawing unit moves, every enemy unit it was engaged with makes a **Parting Strike**: roll one melee attack for each model in base contact with the withdrawing unit, using its best melee weapon. Then the withdrawing unit moves.

## 5. Shooting

### The Shoot Action

A unit cannot Shoot if it is engaged, or if it took two Move actions this activation. To shoot, follow these steps:

**Step 1: Choose a Target**

Pick one enemy unit. At least one model in the shooting unit must be within range of its weapon and have line of sight to at least one model in the target unit. A unit cannot shoot at an enemy unit that is engaged with a friendly unit.

**Step 2: Count Attacks**

Each model in range and with line of sight may fire one ranged weapon. Add up the **Attacks** of all weapons firing. Different weapons are rolled separately.

**Step 3: Roll to Hit**

Roll one D6 for each attack. Every roll that equals or beats the firing model's **Aim** stat is a hit. Apply these modifiers:

| **Situation**                                         | **Modifier** |
|:------------------------------------------------------|:-------------|
| The shooting unit took the Aim action this activation | +1 to hit    |
| Target is more than half the weapon's range away      | –1 to hit    |
| Firing a Heavy weapon after moving this activation    | –1 to hit    |

**Step 4: Target Rolls Saves**

For each hit, the target's player rolls a D6. The target needs to equal or beat its **Armour** stat, made worse by the weapon's **Power**. For example, a model with Armour 3+ hit by a Power 1 weapon saves on 4+.

If the target unit is **in cover** (more than half of its models are inside or behind terrain), it gets **+1** to its saves. If the modified save needed is 7 or more, no save is possible.

**Step 5: Apply Damage**

Each failed save causes damage equal to the weapon's **Damage** stat. The target's controlling player chooses which models take damage, but must keep assigning damage to the same model until it is removed. Damage does not carry over from one model to the next: excess damage on a single model is lost.

> **Worked Example: Shooting**
>
> Five **Varkonian Legionaries** take a Move action, then Shoot at a pack of **Swarmers** 10" away. Volt rifles have the Rapid trait, so each rifle gets 2 attacks at half range or closer: 10 attacks in total.
>
> The Legionaries' Aim is 4+. They roll 10 dice and score 6 hits.
>
> Swarmers have Armour 6+. The volt rifle's Power 1 makes that 7+, so no save is possible. Six Swarmers are removed.

## 6. Melee Combat

### The Charge Action

A unit that is not engaged can Charge an enemy unit within **12"**. A unit cannot Charge if it has already taken two Move actions this activation.

1.  **Declare the target.** Choose one enemy unit within 12".

2.  **Roll for charge distance.** Roll a D6 and add the highest Move stat in the charging unit. This is the charge distance.

3.  **Move.** If the charge distance is enough for at least one model to reach base contact with the target, the charge succeeds. Move each model up to the charge distance, finishing as close to the enemy as possible. At least one model must reach base contact.

4.  **Failed charge.** If the distance is not enough, the unit does not move. Its activation ends.

### The Clash

When a charge succeeds, a **Clash** begins immediately:

1.  **The charger strikes first.** The charging unit makes its melee attacks against the target and gets **+1 to hit** this time, thanks to the impact of the charge.

2.  **The defender strikes back.** Any surviving models in the target unit within 1" of an enemy model then make their melee attacks against the charging unit, even if the target has already activated this round.

The charging unit's activation then ends. Both units are now engaged.

### The Fight Action

In later activations, an engaged unit can take the **Fight** action to attack an enemy unit it is engaged with. Only the active unit attacks. There is no strike back.

### Making Melee Attacks

Every model within **1" of an enemy model**, or within 1" of a friendly model from the same unit that is in base contact with an enemy, may attack. Each model attacks with one melee weapon, rolling dice equal to its **Attacks**.

- **Roll to hit:** each roll that equals or beats the model's **Fight** stat is a hit.

- **Saves and damage:** exactly as in shooting (section 5, steps 4 and 5). Cover gives **no bonus** in melee.

Models without a melee weapon fight with a basic close combat attack: Range Melee, Attacks 1, Power 0, Damage 1.

> **Worked Example: The Clash**
>
> Twelve **Swarmers** charge four **Legionaries** 7" away. Their Move is 8", so any roll succeeds. They reach base contact.
>
> **Charger strikes first.** Ten Swarmers can attack, each with 2 Rending claw attacks: 20 dice. Their Fight is 4+, improved to 3+ for charging. They score 14 hits, and 3 of those rolled a 6. Rending means those 3 hits ignore saves.
>
> The other 11 hits are saved on 3+ (Legionary Armour 3+, claw Power 0). The Legionaries fail 3 saves. With 6 unsaved hits in total, all four Legionaries are removed before they can strike back.

## 7. Weapon Traits

These are the core weapon traits used across all factions. More traits will be added with each army.

| **Trait**        | **Effect**                                                                  |
|:-----------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Rapid**        | The weapon's Attacks are doubled when the target is within half range.      |
| **Heavy**        | –1 to hit if the firing unit took a Move action this activation.            |
| **Pistol**       | Can be fired while engaged, but only at a unit the firer is engaged with.   |
| **Blast**        | +1 Attack for every 5 models in the target unit.                            |
| **Rending**      | Unmodified hit rolls of 6 cause damage automatically, with no save allowed. |
| **Precise**      | The attacker chooses which model in the target unit takes the damage.       |
| **Reach**        | Melee weapon. The model can attack enemies within 2" instead of 1".         |
| **Indirect**     | Can target units that are not in line of sight, with –1 to hit.             |
| **Rift-charged** | Linked to the Rift Energy mechanic. Full rules in Core Rules Part 2.        |

## Quick Reference

### Battle Round

1.  **Initiative:** roll off; the winner chooses who activates first.

2.  **Activation:** alternate activating one unit at a time. Each unit takes 2 actions.

3.  **End:** morale, objectives, remove tokens. (Core Rules Part 2)

### Actions

**Move** (can take twice) • **Shoot** • **Charge** • **Fight** • **Aim** • **Hold** • **Withdraw** (uses both actions)

### Attack Sequence

1.  Roll to hit against **Aim** (ranged) or **Fight** (melee).

2.  The target saves against **Armour**, worsened by **Power**. Cover gives +1 (ranged only).

3.  Each failed save deals **Damage**. The defender assigns damage, finishing one model before the next.
